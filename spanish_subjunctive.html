How to Use This App

What this is

A high-intensity drill tool for one thing: Spanish subjunctive conjugation. The subjunctive is a mood, not a tense — it expresses doubt, emotion, desire, hypotheticals, and things that haven't happened. It's the biggest remaining hurdle after mastering the indicative, and where intermediate learners most often plateau.

This app gives you 39 verbs × 6 pronouns × 4 tenses = 936 cards (702 with the pluperfect tier off). It drills form production — the same principle as the base Spanish app, applied to the subjunctive family.

Think of this app as the second act after the base verb terminal.

What this app is not

It drills how to form the subjunctive, not when to use it. Those are separate skills. You can produce hable perfectly and still use the indicative hablo in a sentence that should trigger the subjunctive. Sentence-level trigger recognition is a different drill and a different app — see the final note.

For a summary of common triggers, see the Conjugation Tables tab (Trigger Phrases section). It's a reference, not a drill.

The Tenses
Present subjunctive

The workhorse. Used for present or future uncertainty: Quiero que hables ("I want you to speak"). Forms from the yo-form of the present indicative, with "opposite vowel" endings: -ar verbs take e-endings (hable), -er/-ir verbs take a-endings (coma, viva). Stem changes follow the boot pattern. Irregular yo-forms carry over (tengo → tenga) — except for the five verbs whose subjunctive is entirely irregular (ser, estar, ir, saber, dar).

Imperfect subjunctive

Used for past uncertainty, hypotheticals, and polite softening: Si hablara... ("If I spoke..."). Forms from the 3rd-person-plural preterite stem + -ra endings: hablaron → hablara, tuvieron → tuviera, dijeron → dijera. Once you know a verb's preterite 3pl, the imperfect subjunctive is mechanical.

Only the -ra form is drilled, not -se (hablase etc). Both are correct; -ra is far more common in modern speech and writing. -se is noted in the conjugation tables but not drilled.

Present perfect subjunctive

Haya/hayas/etc. + past participle: Espero que hayas comido ("I hope you've eaten"). Used for past events with present relevance in a subjunctive clause — the subjunctive equivalent of the indicative present perfect.

Pluperfect subjunctive

Hubiera/hubieras/etc. + past participle: Si hubiera sabido... ("If I had known..."). Used for counterfactual past scenarios. It's the highest tier — off by default. Turn it on in Options when you're ready.

The Verb Classes

The seven class tags in the drawer are designed to teach you why each verb behaves the way it does:

AR / ER / IRFully regular verbs. Present subjunctive uses simple opposite-vowel endings; imperfect subjunctive follows the 3pl preterite stem. These are your baseline.
SPLSpelling-change verbs (buscar, llegar, empezar). The letter before the ending shifts to preserve the sound — c→qu (busque), g→gu (llegue), z→c (empiece). Affects only the present subjunctive; imperfect is unaffected.
STEMStem-changing verbs. The boot pattern: the stem changes in stressed forms, reverts in nosotros/vosotros. For -ir verbs (dormir, sentir, pedir), the nosotros/vosotros shift takes a different vowel (durmamos, sintamos) — a distinctive marker. Jugar and seguir combine a stem change with a spelling change (juegue, siga).
GOVerbs whose subjunctive derives from an irregular yo-form ending in -go or -zco: tener (tengo → tenga), hacer (hago → haga), decir (digo → diga), venir (vengo → venga), poner (pongo → ponga), salir (salgo → salga), conocer (conozco → conozca), oír (oigo → oiga). The subjunctive stem is the yo-form minus -o.
IRREGVerbs whose subjunctive is not derived from the yo-form: ser (sea), estar (esté), ir (vaya), saber (sepa), dar (dé). Five verbs to memorise — that's the whole list.
The Settings
Adaptive vs Full Sweep

Adaptive (default) draws from Active + Pending + Pinned, skipping Known. Full Sweep draws from everything, useful for a check-up. Failing a Known card in Sweep demotes it back to Pending.

Production vs acoustic
  • Production Lane — you see the English gloss and target tense, you type the conjugated form.
  • Acoustic Lane — you hear the form, you identify person and tense. Self-assess (honour).
Accent handling

The subjunctive has multiple accent-bearing forms: habléis, comáis, tengáis (present vosotros); habláramos, comiéramos (imperfect nosotros); hayáis, hubiéramos (compound). Lenient mode forgives them; strict requires them.

Suggested Workflow

Beginners to subjunctive. Start with Present Subjunctive only (uncheck imperfect, perfect, pluperfect). Filter to AR + ER + IR regular verbs. Get the basic endings automatic before adding stem-changers or irregulars.

Add the irregular yo-form family. Turn on the GO class. The pattern is uniform (yo-form minus -o + endings), so if you know tengo → tenga, you can extend to vengo, pongo, salgo.

Add the five IRREG verbs. Ser, estar, ir, saber, dar. These must be memorised; there's no shortcut. Do them one at a time.

Add stem-changers. Pay particular attention to Dormir and Sentir — the nosotros vowel shift (durmamos, sintamos) is the most distinctive subjunctive pattern in Spanish.

Add Imperfect Subjunctive. This is nearly free once the preterite is solid — just 3pl preterite stem + -ra. Watch the y-insertion verbs (Leer, Oír) and the 3pl-shift verbs (Dormir, Pedir, Sentir).

Add compound tenses. Present perfect subjunctive is Tier 2. Pluperfect is Tier 3 — off by default. Turn it on when the earlier tenses feel solid.

Every third or fourth session. Hit Drill Pending in the Reviews tab. This is where you patch the holes.

When you're tired. Turn on 🎮 Play Mode. Nothing gets recorded.

Final Note

This app drills the four subjunctive tenses. Not covered: the -se variant of the imperfect subjunctive (hablase — see conjugation tables), the future subjunctive (fuere — archaic, only legal texts), or the imperative (a separate mood). Sentence-level trigger selection — deciding whether a clause needs subjunctive at all — is a separate skill and a separate app's worth of content. Use this to get the forms automatic; use the trigger-phrase reference in the Conjugation Tables to recognise when to reach for them.

Collisions & Ambiguities

OverlapDetail
yo = él / ellaSame form in every subjunctive tense. This is why the pronoun is always shown in the prompt.
ser = ir (imperfect)Both are fuera, fueras, fuera, fuéramos, fuerais, fueran — because they share preterite forms (fui, fuiste...). This is why the verb is always shown.
-ra vs -seOnly -ra is drilled. The -se variant (hablase, comiese) is equally correct but less common in modern speech.
Subjunctive vs conditionalImperfect subjunctive hablara and conditional hablaría pair in conditionals: Si hablara, hablaría. Both drill differently; the conditional lives in the base verb app.
