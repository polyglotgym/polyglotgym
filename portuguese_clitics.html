How to Use This App

What this is

A drill for Portuguese clitic pronouns — the small pronouns (me, te, o, a, lhe, nos) that replace objects. Portuguese placement is the opposite of Spanish for finite verbs: European Portuguese normally puts the clitic after the verb (compro-o), not before (~~o compro~~). It also has mesoclisis (dar-te-ei) and a full contraction system (mo, lho, no-lo), neither of which Spanish has.

The Portuguese Clitic System
Direct vs indirect pronouns

Direct objects answer "what?" — o, a, os, as ("it / them"). Indirect objects answer "to/for whom?" — lhe, lhes ("to him/her/them"). First- and second-person pronouns (me, te, nos, vos) work as both.

When they combine, indirect goes first and contracts (see the Contractions section below).

Placement: the Portuguese inversion

This is the biggest adjustment for a Spanish speaker. In European Portuguese:

Default placement is enclisis — the clitic comes after the verb. Compro-o, not o compro.

Proclisis (before the verb) happens only when triggered:

  • Negation: não o compro
  • Subordinating conjunction (que, se, porque, quando): sei que o vi
  • WH-words (quem, que, onde, quando): quem te disse?
  • Quantified subject (todos, alguém, ninguém): todos se lavaram
  • Certain adverbs (sempre, também, já, ainda): sempre te disse

The deck drills the default enclisis for statements and imperatives, proclisis for negatives (via não), and the future/conditional rule (mesoclisis) separately.

The allomorph rule

o/a/os/as take different forms after certain endings:

  • After a vowel: keep o/a/os/as — compro-o, dá-o, compra-o
  • After -r, -s, or -z: become lo/la/los/las, with the consonant dropped — comprar → comprá-lo, diz → di-lo, traz → trá-lo
  • After a nasal (-ão, -õe, -m): become no/na/nos/nas — põe → põe-no, tem → tem-no

lhe/lhes never change form. They just hyphenate: dou-lhe, diz-lhes.

Contractions

Indirect + direct combine into a single cluster:

  • me + o → mo ("it to me")
  • te + o → to ("it to you")
  • lhe + o → lho (also lhes + o → lho)
  • nos + o → no-lo ("it to us")
  • vos + o → vo-lo (archaic — reference only)

Affirmative: hyphenate to the verb — dá-mo, diz-lho. Negative: two words after não — não mo dês, não lho digas.

Mesoclisis

In the future and conditional, when no proclisis trigger is present, the clitic goes inside the verb:

  • dar-te-ei / dar-te-ia (with lhe: no r-drop)
  • comprá-lo-ei / comprá-lo-ia (with o: drop r, acute accent, -lo)
  • di-lo-ei (dizer, irregular stem + r-drop)
  • fá-lo-ei (fazer, irregular stem + r-drop)
  • trá-lo-ei (trazer, irregular stem + r-drop)
  • tê-lo-ei (ter, closed ê, r-drop)

With a proclisis trigger, proclisis wins: não te darei, not não dar-te-ei.

Modal + infinitive

With querer or poder + infinitive, both placements are valid:

  • Quero comprá-lo (attached to infinitive)
  • Quero-o comprar (before the infinitive)

Modal cards accept either. Feedback shows both. With two-clitic modals (Quero dar-mo), only the attached form is drilled — the proclitic double-placement is very awkward and rare.

The tú / você trap

The deck drills tu imperative forms throughout. Tu takes 2nd-person clitics (te, to, ta). Você is grammatically third-person, so it takes third-person clitics (o, a, lhe).

  • "I see you (tu)" = Vejo-te
  • "I see you (você)" = Vejo-o (not ~~Vejo-te~~)

English speakers reach for te when they hear "you", but você demands third-person forms. The deck drills tu only, so you'll build the tu habit first. The guide flags the você trap for when you meet it in the wild.

European vs Brazilian

This app drills European Portuguese. Brazilian Portuguese uses a different placement system:

  • BP default is proclisis in speech: Me dá isso, not Dá-me isso
  • Enclisis sounds literary in BP
  • Mesoclisis is essentially absent from modern BP
  • The contraction system (mo, lho) is less common in speech

If you're learning BP: mentally map EP enclisis → BP proclisis. The forms are largely the same; only the placement differs. The reference table shows both defaults.

vós

vós is archaic in EP (liturgy and some dialects) and absent from BP. The reference tables show the contractions (vo-lo, vo-la); the deck doesn't drill them.

Trap cards

By default, trap cards show a generic "⚠️ Trap card" badge before you answer. Turn on Detailed trap hints to see the specific trap named. Feedback always shows the full explanation.

What's not covered
  • Reflexive verbs as objects (lavar-se) — separate skill
  • Se impersonal / passive (vende-se) — different grammar
  • Possessives (vosso/vossa) — not clitics
  • Inflected infinitive with clitics — reference only
  • BP substitutions (a gente, você, vocês) — guide only
Suggested Workflow

Start with Direct. Get the enclisis default automatic and the -lo/-no allomorphs familiar.

Then Indirect. lhe/lhes don't change form, so it's cleaner. Focus on placement.

Then Contractions. The mo/lho/no-lo grid is the biggest mental jump.

Then Mesoclisis. Future/conditional only; r-drop for o/a; no drop for lhe.

Then Modal. Both placements valid. This category is the most "advanced" only because it combines everything else.

Every third or fourth session. Hit Drill Pending in the Reviews tab.

When you're tired. Turn on 🎮 Play Mode. Nothing gets recorded.

The Traps

dá-o, not dá-lo: the -lo allomorph only appears after -r/-s/-z. dá ends in a stressed vowel.
di-lo but diz-mo: -z drops before l of -lo; kept before m of contraction.
trá-lo but traz-mo: same pattern.
põe-no / tem-no: nasal endings trigger no/na/nos/nas, not lo/la.
Mesoclisis r-drop: comprá-lo-ei (not comprar-lo-ei).
tê-lo: closed ê (from ter + -lo). Strict mode requires it.
Tu ≠ você: você is grammatically third-person — takes o/a/lhe, not te.

European vs Brazilian

This deck drills EP. BP differs in placement, not form: EP uses enclisis as default, BP uses proclisis. Mesoclisis is essentially absent from modern BP speech. Contractions are less common in BP. If learning BP, mentally map EP enclisis → BP proclisis.
