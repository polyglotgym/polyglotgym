How to Use This App

What this is

A high-intensity drill tool for one thing: Japanese verb conjugation. Not vocabulary, not kanji readings, not politeness strategies — just form production.

Verb conjugation is the central mechanical problem in Japanese. The tense system is simple (non-past / past), but the sound changes — te-form, ta-form, the godan/ichidan split, stem shifts — trip up every learner at some point. This app gives you 20 verbs × 17 forms = 340 cards to drill until it's automatic.

Think of this app as like a metronome for verb conjugation.

How this differs from the other suite apps

Japanese has no grammatical person and no gender — no pronouns to conjugate against. The card model is therefore simpler: (verb × form). What the drill tests is form production, given the dictionary form and the form label.

Japanese also has no future tense. The dictionary form covers both present habitual and future. The "polite" register (masu) is baked into the form, not a separate toggle.

The four piles
⚪ ActiveCards you're still working through. Appear in every drill. A card is Active if it's new or if you've failed it once and corrected it.
🟡 PendingCards you've recently missed. Mixed into the general rotation but called out separately. Use Drill Pending to focus on them. Pin with 📍 or 📌 All.

Japanese-Specific Behaviour
The three verb classes

Godan (五段, "five-row") — most verbs. The ending consonant changes across forms: 書く → 書かない → 書いて. Nine ending types (-く, -ぐ, -す, -つ, -ぬ, -ぶ, -む, -う, -る) with distinct sound-change rules.

Ichidan (一段, "one-row") — stem stays fixed; the る is dropped and various endings added: 食べる → 食べない → 食べて. Simpler mechanically, but you have to distinguish them from godan -る verbs like 取る.

Irregular — する, 来る, and 行く (godan but irregular te/past).

Scripts

Answers are accepted in kanji (書いた), hiragana (かいた), or romaji (kaita). Romaji accepts both Hepburn (shi, chi, tsu, fu) and Kunrei-shiki (si, ti, tu, hu) spellings, plus macron long vowels (kō, kyū). Spaces are ignored.

The Script Display settings in the drill drawer control how the verb identifier appears in prompts. If you can't yet read kanji, toggle Kanji off. If you're still learning kana, toggle Romaji on. The answer feedback always shows all three scripts regardless of these settings, so you're always reinforcing the association.

Te-form

Te-form is the single hardest form for learners, so every te-form card shows the sound-change rule (e.g., "く → いて"). This is your reference — read it as a reminder rather than a hint.

Politeness

The polite forms are drilled alongside their plain equivalents. No separate toggle — if you want to practice only plain, filter out the polite forms in the settings.

Dictionary form cards

Dictionary form cards are special: since the dictionary form is the answer, the prompt shows only the English meaning ("to write"), not the verb. You have to recall the dictionary form from the meaning. Every other card shows the dictionary form as context.

The Settings
Adaptive vs Full Sweep

Adaptive (default) draws from Active + Pending + Pinned, skipping Known. Full Sweep draws from everything, useful for a check-up. Failing a Known card in Sweep demotes it back to Pending.

Production vs acoustic
  • Production Lane — you see the verb and form, you type the answer.
  • Acoustic Lane — you hear the conjugated form, you identify the form. Then self-assess (honour).
Filters

You can filter by verb ending class (nine godan ending types, ichidan, irregular) and by form tier (three tiers of increasing complexity). You can also isolate a single verb for targeted practice.

Drilling
Reading the scoreboard
  • Correct / Checked — lifetime totals.
  • Streak — current streak; 👑 = 15+ in a row.
  • Known: X / Y — under current filters.
  • Progress: X / Y — current session only.
Hotkeys

Space or Enter for move on / correct, X (or 1) for incorrect, 2 for correct (honour mode), Ctrl+Z to undo the last answer.

Suggested Workflow

Beginners. Start with the core forms only (uncheck Common and Advanced tiers). Focus on ichidan verbs first — they're mechanically simple and let you learn the masu/past/te forms without worrying about sound changes. Then move to godan -す and -く, the most regular godan endings.

Upper beginners. Add godan in waves — the -つ/-る/-う (っ-te) group first, then -む/-ぶ/-ぬ (ん-te), then -ぐ (いで, the odd one out). Bring in te-form, potential, ba-conditional from Tier 2.

Intermediate. Add want-to, volitional, polite volitional. Add the irregulars する and 来る. Once comfortable, drill Full Sweep to test retention across all forms.

Advanced. Turn on passive, causative, imperative. Use Drill Pending in Reviews to patch holes.

When tired. Turn on 🎮 Play Mode — nothing gets recorded.

Final Note

This app covers 20 verbs across 17 forms. Not covered: ら抜き (colloquial potential, e.g. 食べれる), せよ (literary imperative of する), or the subjunctive-equivalent conditionals (たら, なら). Each is a natural follow-up once the core set is automatic.

Collisions & Ambiguities

OverlapForm(s)
Ichidan passive = potential食べられる is both "is eaten" and "can eat" — same form, distinct concept.
Non-past covers present + future書く = "writes" or "will write" depending on context.
Godan -る vs ichidanBoth end in る in the dictionary form, but conjugate differently (取る → 取らない vs 食べる → 食べない). Memory aid: if the -eru/-iru sound is more common among ichidan, but exceptions abound (帰る, 切る are godan despite -iru).

Notes

Imperative する: しろ is standard; せよ is literary/formal, not drilled.
ら抜き (colloquial potential): 食べれる / 見れる are common in speech but not drilled here.
Polite forms: All derive from the masu-stem (書き-, 食べ-, し-, 来(き)-). Once you know that stem, the entire polite family follows.
来る: The stem shifts between き- (polite/past/te), こ- (negative/potential/volitional/imperative), く (dictionary), and くれ (ba-conditional). Each form's hint tells you which stem to use.
