How to Use This App

What this is

A high-intensity drill tool for one thing: Italian verb conjugation. Not vocabulary. Not a course. Not spaced repetition.

Verb conjugation is the central problem to gaining Italian fluency and this app aims to help you solve that problem. If you cannot conjugate verbs quickly, your speech will falter. If you get them wrong, people will not know who is doing what.

If you have ever looked at endless tables of verb conjugations in the back of a textbook and thought "How am I going to remember all of this?!", then this app is for you. It contains the core set of verb endings you need to drill — roughly 800 cards — and it will help you master them efficiently.

Think of this app as like a metronome for verb conjugation.

How this differs from Anki

I made this app to feel like a flexible set of paper flashcards in your hand that you can slice up and study at your own pace. On the face of it, it is similar to flashcard/SRS apps like Anki; however, there are key differences.

Firstly, I use and recommend Anki. Anki's spacing algorithm is excellent for vocabulary: tens of thousands of items, each getting progressively less frequent as you learn it. It's also easy to add your own words. What's more, it's free!

That said, I have found that there are issues with Anki. In Anki, you do not control the algorithm and it can be over or underwhelming (too frequent or not enough). Core high-percentage cards are treated the same as less useful words. Verb endings need massed practice. You need to see parlo, parli, parla, parliamo, parlate, parlano, etc., repeatedly as a beginner — not once every three weeks.

Unlike Anki, this app will not double your review pile if you miss a day. You may do as many study sessions as you like, whenever you like, without fear that tomorrow's workload will become unbearable. However, this app does record your mistakes, so that you can come back to them. You can pin difficult verb forms. This app will never remove "leech" cards from the deck.

The Idea
The four piles

The Reviews tab shows four piles. Each one has a purpose:

⚪ ActiveCards you're still working through. These appear in every drill session. A card is Active if it's new (never shown) or if you've failed it once and corrected it. Both kinds live here because they need the same thing: one clean pass to graduate.
🟡 PendingCards you've recently missed. These also appear in drill sessions, mixed in with Active, but they're called out separately because you can drill them deliberately. Pending cards are rare in the general rotation, so the Drill Pending button exists to bring them to the front. Pin individually with 📍, or use the 📌 All button above the table to pin every pending card at once. Clicking ✖ on any row removes it from the log entirely — the card returns to Active.
🔴 PinnedYour watchlist. These stay in the deck indefinitely and never auto-clear. Add a card by clicking 📍 on any row, or let the auto-pin threshold do it (default: after 3 total fails). To retire a pinned card, correct it, then unpin it manually.
💚 KnownMastered. Once a card is Known, it's skipped in Adaptive mode. To see it again, switch to Full Sweep, or use Forget All to reset the whole known pile.
How cards move between piles

Cards change state based on your first answer in each session — not on the tenth attempt after five wrong ones:

FromCorrect (first attempt)Wrong
Active→ Known→ Pending
Pending→ Active→ Pending
Pinned→ Pinned (stays)→ Pinned (stays)
Known→ Known (stays)→ Pending (Sweep only)

If you fail a card and then get it right later in the same session, that later correct answer doesn't count for state changes — it's just practice. The state already moved when you failed.

The feedback message after each answer tells you exactly what happened: "↩️ Moved back to Active deck", "✨ Graduated to Known", "🔴 Auto-pinned (fail #3)", and so on.

The Settings
The two modes

Adaptive (default) — draws only from Active + Pending + Pinned. Skips Known. This is the mode you'll use most of the time; it's how you grind through the deck.

Full Sweep — draws from every card, including Known. Use it for a check-up, or to see how much you've retained. Failing a Known card in Sweep demotes it back to Pending.

If in doubt, leave it on Adaptive, and check your Pending pile every so often.

Production vs acoustic

The two buttons below the main tabs switch how the card is presented:

  • Production Lane — you see the English gloss and type the Italian form. This is where you produce.
  • Acoustic Lane — you hear the Italian form and identify which person and tense it is. This is where you recognise.

The app does not distinguish between the two for progress tracking. A card is one card: if you get parlo right in production, it counts the same as getting it right acoustically. One mental form, one card. A miss in either mode counts as a miss. You can type the ending in Production or use the "Honour" mode (self-assessment — be honest!). Acoustic is honour mode only.

If you want to test a specific modality only — say acoustically — switch to Acoustic Lane, turn on Play Mode, and drill. Nothing is recorded, but you get the practice.

Speaker-gender toggle

Some forms change if the speaker is female (e.g. partito vs partita) — toggle as appropriate for your needs.

Deck size

Change the deck size in the drawer for shorter or longer sessions — 15 is a good default; 50 makes a focused study block.

Drilling
Reading the scoreboard
  • Correct / Checked — lifetime totals. Every answer, every session. The ratio is your lifetime accuracy.
  • Streak — current streak. Resets on any wrong answer; 👑 means 15+ in a row.
  • Known: 62 / 864 — progress under your current filters. Narrow the filters and both numbers shrink: it's telling you how far through this slice you are, not the whole deck.
  • Progress: 6 / 6 — current session only. Resets every session. The right number grows if you fail cards (they get pushed to the back of the deck).

That's the whole model. No intervals, no "again / hard / good / easy", no scheduling algorithm deciding when you should see a card. You decide the pace; the app tracks the state.

Hotkeys

Space or Enter for move on / correct, X for incorrect.

Ctrl+Z undoes the last answer for the scoreboard and session queue. It does not reverse log changes (Pending/Pinned/Known) — those stay as recorded.

Suggested Workflow

Beginners. Select the verbs, tenses, and pronouns that you want to master from the options. You can choose specific verbs using "isolate specific verb". Start small. Unclick the Passato Remoto tense (it's mostly literary). Use the Acoustic mode (listening) if you prefer. Move on when you feel ready.

Upper beginners. Select a wider range of verbs, tenses, and pronouns. Try to produce the correct forms.

Intermediate. Open up the whole deck, choose your deck size, and see where the holes are in your verb conjugation. Try the Passato Remoto.

Consistency is the way to make progress — try to drill a session each day. One 15-card Adaptive session of 5–10 minutes will cover the whole deck in a couple of months.

Every third or fourth session. Instead of the normal Adaptive session, go to Reviews and hit Drill Pending. This is where you patch the holes. Pending cards don't come up often enough in the general rotation, so drilling them deliberately matters.

Occasionally. Drill Pinned if it's growing. Run Full Sweep if you want a spot-check. Use the filters to isolate a specific tense or verb class when you want a targeted session.

When you're tired. Turn on 🎮 Play Mode. Nothing gets recorded — no state changes, no log entries, no known cards added. Useful for a low-stakes warm-up, or for testing yourself without committing (especially useful on unknown verb tenses).

Remember, there are no rules (other than grammatical ones), so you could drill the whole deck in a day if you really want to!

Why no continuous tenses?

You may notice that stare + gerund forms (sto parlando, stavo lavorando, etc.) are absent from the deck. This is deliberate, on three grounds.

1. English speakers overuse them. Where English distinguishes "I work" from "I'm working", Italian usually uses the simple present for both: lavoro covers both senses. Translating "I'm working" word-for-word to sto lavorando is one of the most common learner errors. Drilling the continuous would strengthen the habit we're trying to break.

2. The continuous is compositional. Its form is stare + gerund (-ando for -ARE verbs, -endo for -ERE/-IRE verbs). Once you know both halves, you can build every continuous form. Drilling the composite adds cards without adding knowledge.

3. Deck size. Adding two continuous tenses (presente, imperfetto) × 6 pronouns × 24 verbs would add roughly 290 cards — about a third more — for a form the learner mostly shouldn't be using.

Why include passato remoto but not this? Fair challenge, but the situations aren't parallel. Passato remoto has its own endings; if you don't drill them, you can't produce them. The continuous has no new endings — it borrows stare's and the gerund's. That's why the payoff is genuinely different.

When the continuous is correct. It emphasises that an action is happening right now (adesso sta piovendo), or contrasts with a general statement (di solito lavoro a casa, ma oggi sto lavorando in ufficio). The choice is contextual rather than structural, so it can't easily be drilled as a form. Read and listen for the difference; don't drill it. Italian's continuous is if anything even more restricted than Spanish's — parlo is the overwhelming default, and sto parlando is reserved for genuine right-now emphasis.

Final Note

Regarding the compound tenses, this app covers the auxiliary verbs avere and essere in the compound past (passato prossimo), but does not include: 1) other compound tenses (these are relatively simple once you have mastered avere/essere & participles) or 2) the subjunctive forms. Adding all the compound tenses would have increased the deck size to an unreasonable degree. The subjunctive will probably require another app.
