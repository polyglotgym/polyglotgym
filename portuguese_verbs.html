How to Use This App

What this is

A high-intensity drill tool for one thing: Portuguese verb conjugation. Not vocabulary. Not a course. Not spaced repetition.

Verb conjugation is the central problem to gaining Portuguese fluency and this app aims to help you solve that problem. If you cannot conjugate verbs quickly, your speech will falter. If you get them wrong, people will not know who is doing what.

If you have ever looked at endless tables of verb conjugations in the back of a textbook and thought "How am I going to remember all of this?!", then this app is for you. It contains the core set of verb endings you need to drill — roughly 900 cards — and it will help you master them efficiently.

Think of this app as like a metronome for verb conjugation.

How this differs from Anki

Anki's spacing algorithm is excellent for vocabulary: tens of thousands of items, each getting progressively less frequent as you learn it. It's also easy to add your own words. What's more, it's free!

That said, I have found that there are issues with Anki. In Anki, you do not control the algorithm and it can be over or underwhelming (too frequent or not enough). Core high-percentage cards are treated the same as less useful words. Verb endings need massed practice. You need to see falo, falas, fala, falamos, falais, falam, etc., repeatedly as a beginner — not once every three weeks.

Unlike Anki, this app will not double your review pile if you miss a day. You may do as many study sessions as you like, whenever you like, without fear that tomorrow's workload will become unbearable. However, this app does record your mistakes, so that you can come back to them. You can pin difficult verb forms. This app will never remove "leech" cards from the deck.

The Idea
The four piles

The Reviews tab shows four piles. Each one has a purpose:

⚪ ActiveCards you're still working through. These appear in every drill session. A card is Active if it's new (never shown) or if you've failed it once and corrected it. Both kinds live here because they need the same thing: one clean pass to graduate.
🟡 PendingCards you've recently missed. These also appear in drill sessions, mixed in with Active, but they're called out separately because you can drill them deliberately. Pending cards are rare in the general rotation, so the Drill Pending button exists to bring them to the front. Pin individually with 📍, or use the 📌 All button above the table to pin every pending card at once. Clicking ✖ on any row removes it from the log entirely — the card returns to Active.
🔴 PinnedYour watchlist. These stay in the deck indefinitely and never auto-clear. Add a card by clicking 📍 on any row, or let the auto-pin threshold do it (default: after 3 total fails). To retire a pinned card, correct it, then unpin it manually.
💚 KnownMastered. Once a card is Known, it's skipped in Adaptive mode. To see it again, switch to Full Sweep, or use Forget All to reset the whole known pile.
How cards move between piles

Cards change state based on your first answer in each session — not on the tenth attempt after five wrong ones:

FromCorrect (first attempt)Wrong

Pending→ Active→ Pending

If you fail a card and then get it right later in the same session, that later correct answer doesn't count for state changes — it's just practice. The state already moved when you failed.

The feedback message after each answer tells you exactly what happened: "↩️ Moved back to Active deck", "✨ Graduated to Known", "🔴 Auto-pinned (fail #3)", and so on.

The Settings
The two modes

Adaptive (default) — draws only from Active + Pending + Pinned. Skips Known. This is the mode you'll use most of the time; it's how you grind through the deck.

Full Sweep — draws from every card, including Known. Use it for a check-up, or to see how much you've retained. Failing a Known card in Sweep demotes it back to Pending.

If in doubt, leave it on Adaptive, and check your Pending pile every so often.

Production vs acoustic

The two buttons below the main tabs switch how the card is presented:

  • Production Lane — you see the English gloss and type the Portuguese form. This is where you produce.
  • Acoustic Lane — you hear the Portuguese form and identify which person and tense it is. This is where you recognise.

The app does not distinguish between the two for progress tracking. A card is one card: if you get falo right in production, it counts the same as getting it right acoustically. One mental form, one card. A miss in either mode counts as a miss. You can type the ending in Production or use the "Honour" mode (self-assessment — be honest!). Acoustic is honour mode only.

If you want to test a specific modality only — say acoustically — switch to Acoustic Lane, turn on Play Mode, and drill. Nothing is recorded, but you get the practice.

Accent handling

Portuguese accents are load-bearing — falamos (presente nós) and falámos (pretérito nós, European convention) are distinct forms; avô (grandfather) and avó (grandmother) differ only by accent. Two modes are available:

  • Lenient (default) — accents, tildes, and cedillas forgiven. Typing falamos passes for both tenses. Convenient for casual drilling.
  • Strict — accents required. Typing falamos for falámos is marked wrong. Essential if accent marks are your weak point.

The homograph warning ("⚠️ Same form also: …") always compares strict forms — so falamos (presente) and falámos (pretérito, when accented) are correctly distinguished.

European vs Brazilian Portuguese

This app follows European Portuguese conventions: it uses tu (informal you) and includes vós (archaic/literary plural you) for completeness. Brazilian Portuguese typically replaces tu with você (3rd-person forms) and vós with vocês (3rd-person plural). If you're learning Brazilian, mentally map tu → você and vós → vocês, or use the filters to ignore these pronouns. The verb forms themselves are valid in both dialects (especially tu forms, which are used in Rio Grande do Sul and the Northeast of Brazil).

Stem-changing verbs

Portuguese has one category of stem-changing verbs: -IR verbs only. The change appears in the eu form of the presente — sirvo (from servir), durmo (from dormir), sinto (from sentir), prefiro (from preferir). The rest of the paradigm is regular.

This is different from Spanish, where the "boot" affects four forms (yo/tú/él/ellos). In Portuguese, it's only the first person singular. The Stem-change filter isolates these four verbs so you can drill them deliberately. When a stem change applies, the answer must be the full form — typing only the ending wouldn't demonstrate that you know the change.

Deck size

Change the deck size in the drawer for shorter or longer sessions — 15 is a good default; 50 makes a focused study block.

Drilling
Reading the scoreboard
  • Correct / Checked — lifetime totals. Every answer, every session. The ratio is your lifetime accuracy.
  • Streak — current streak. Resets on any wrong answer; 👑 means 15+ in a row.
  • Known: 62 / 936 — progress under your current filters. Narrow the filters and both numbers shrink: it's telling you how far through this slice you are, not the whole deck.
  • Progress: 6 / 6 — current session only. Resets every session. The right number grows if you fail cards (they get pushed to the back of the deck).

That's the whole model. No intervals, no "again / hard / good / easy", no scheduling algorithm deciding when you should see a card. You decide the pace; the app tracks the state.

Hotkeys

Space or Enter for move on / correct, X (or 1) for incorrect, 2 for correct (honour mode).

Ctrl+Z undoes the last answer for the scoreboard and session queue. It does not reverse log changes (Pending/Pinned/Known) — those stay as recorded.

Suggested Workflow

Beginners. Select the verbs, tenses, and pronouns that you want to master from the options. You can choose specific verbs using "isolate specific verb". Start small. Use the Acoustic mode (listening) if you prefer. Move on when you feel ready.

Upper beginners. Select a wider range of verbs, tenses, and pronouns. Try to produce the correct forms.

Intermediate. Open up the whole deck, choose your deck size, and see where the holes are. Try a session dedicated to stem-changers or irregular verbs.

Consistency is the way to make progress — try to drill a session each day. One 15-card Adaptive session of 5–10 minutes will cover the whole deck in a couple of months.

Every third or fourth session. Instead of the normal Adaptive session, go to Reviews and hit Drill Pending. This is where you patch the holes. Pending cards don't come up often enough in the general rotation, so drilling them deliberately matters.

Occasionally. Drill Pinned if it's growing. Run Full Sweep if you want a spot-check. Use the filters to isolate a specific tense or verb class when you want a targeted session.

When you're tired. Turn on 🎮 Play Mode. Nothing gets recorded — no state changes, no log entries, no known cards added. Useful for a low-stakes warm-up, or for testing yourself without committing (especially useful on unknown verb tenses).

Remember, there are no rules (other than grammatical ones), so you could drill the whole deck in a day if you really want to!

Why no continuous tenses?

You may notice that progressive forms are absent from the deck. This is deliberate, on three grounds.

1. English speakers overuse them. Where English distinguishes "I work" from "I'm working", Portuguese usually uses the simple present for both: trabalho covers both senses. Translating "I'm working" word-for-word to estou a trabalhar (European) or estou trabalhando (Brazilian) is one of the most common learner errors. Drilling the continuous would strengthen the habit we're trying to break.

2. The continuous is compositional. Its form is estar (already drilled) + a + infinitive (European: estou a falar) or + gerund (Brazilian: estou falando). Once you know estar and the infinitive, you can build every continuous form. Drilling the composite adds cards without adding knowledge.

3. Deck size. Adding two continuous tenses (presente, imperfeito) × 6 pronouns × 26 verbs would add roughly 300 cards — about a third more — for a form the learner mostly shouldn't be using.

When the continuous is correct. It emphasises that an action is happening right now (agora está a chover / agora está chovendo), or contrasts with a general statement (normalmente trabalho em casa, mas hoje estou a trabalhar no escritório). The choice is contextual rather than structural, so it can't easily be drilled as a form. Read and listen for the difference; don't drill it.

Final Note

Why ter and not haver? The compound past in this app uses ter + past participle (tenho falado), which is the standard auxiliary in both European and Brazilian Portuguese, in speech and in most modern writing. Haver as a compound auxiliary (hei falado) is archaic and literary; you'll find it in older texts, but it's not the form you need for fluency. The past participle with ter is invariable — it always ends in -o — so there's no gender or number agreement to worry about.

Regarding the compound tenses, this app covers the auxiliary verb ter in the pretérito perfeito composto (tenho falado, tens falado, etc.), but does not include: 1) other compound tenses (these are relatively simple once you have mastered ter + participles) or 2) the subjunctive forms. Adding all the compound tenses would have increased the deck size to an unreasonable degree. The subjunctive will probably require another app.

A note on syncretism. Portuguese has one genuine collision in the regular paradigm: the 1st-person plural present and preterite are identical for all three classes (falamos, comemos, partimos). In European Portuguese, an acute accent distinguishes the preterite falámos from the present falamos for -AR verbs only; -ER and -IR verbs share the form unambiguously. This app's homograph warning will flag the ambiguity whenever it occurs. In Strict accent mode you'll be required to produce the European accented form; in Lenient mode either spelling is accepted.
